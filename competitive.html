Where We Stand

Odysseon sells a managed mandate running one systematic momentum strategy to high net worth and accredited investors. In that market we are not competing against other quant funds. We are competing against a private bank statement, a robo-advisor, and the investor doing it themselves at a broker.

Against all three we are cheaper to beat on evidence than on price. The private bank reports quarterly and will not show the reasoning behind a position. The robo-advisor is transparent but deliberately passive, so it cannot claim a return that would interest this audience. The self-directed investor has no fee at all, which makes price a losing argument and performance the only one that matters.

Our weakness is the one thing none of that fixes. Two closed positions is not a track record, and most allocators in this bracket screen on three years before they read anything else.

Key findingsImplication
The real incumbent is inertia, not a rival fundMost of this audience is self-directed or sitting in a bank mandate they have not reviewed. The sale is a switch, not a comparison.
Nobody in the regional set competes on per-position transparencyThe Ledger format is the differentiator. It is also free to produce, since we write it anyway.
Track record length is the binding constraintNo pricing or positioning decision can fix it. It resolves with time and published trades, or not at all.
Priority recommendationsWhy
Lead with the Ledger, not the backtestA backtest is the one claim every competitor can also make. A published, dated, per-position record is not.
Lead with the hurdle, not the headline feeAgainst a self-directed investor paying nothing, a flat management fee is the hardest number to defend. At 1 and 12 over a 12 per cent hurdle, the honest pitch is that a year we do not earn costs them 1 per cent.
Target the segment that will read trades instead of waiting three yearsSurvey question 13 sizes it. If that segment is too small, the honest answer is to keep trading and publishing rather than to raise now.

Our structure is a 1 per cent management fee and a 12 per cent performance fee charged only on the return above a 12 per cent hurdle. That puts our flat cost at or above Sarwa and roughly level with a private bank, with the difference that in a year we do not clear 12 per cent the client pays us nothing beyond the 1 per cent. Private bank rates are negotiated per client and not published, so stating a figure for them would be a guess presented as research.

SWOT

Strengths

  • A rules-based strategy with the exit written before the entry, which removes the failure mode that costs self-directed investors the most.
  • Per-position published reporting that no regional competitor currently offers.
  • A full year of backtest across 2,049 trades with a 2.63 per cent maximum drawdown.
  • Two closed positions, both profitable, both documented to the fill.
  • Low cost base, so the business does not need scale to survive.

Weaknesses

  • Two closed positions is not a track record by any institutional standard.
  • A single strategy, so a regime that suppresses volatility compression breakouts suppresses the whole book.
  • No distribution, no brand recognition and no referral network.
  • Fee structure and minimum allocation are still undecided.
  • The option overlay carries far more variance than the spot strategy it is layered on.

Opportunities

  • No competitor in the regional set treats transparency as the product rather than a compliance obligation.
  • ADGM has lowered the practical cost of being a licensed manager, and over 110 now operate there.
  • Investors who have already shopped for a manager and stayed put are the warmest segment and nobody is speaking to them specifically.
  • Self-directed investors pay no fee but keep no record, and the Ledger format speaks directly to that gap.

Threats

  • Allocators who screen on a three year minimum cannot be converted at any price this year.
  • The transparency advantage is copyable. A larger manager could publish the same format and bring a decade of history to it.
  • The 2026 FSRA amendments tighten requirements and could raise the cost of the structure we end up needing.
  • A drawdown materially worse than the backtested 2.63 per cent, early and in public, would be very difficult to recover from with no history behind it.
10

Positioning

The Map

Plotted on the two axes that actually decide this purchase: what the investor pays, and how much return the offering is willing to aim at. The top right quadrant is empty in this market, and that is the whole opportunity.

At 1 and 12 over a 12 per cent hurdle we sit just inside the private bank on fee and well above everything on the board for return ambition, which is the quadrant we wanted. The hurdle is what makes that position defensible: it is the only structure on this map where a bad year costs the client almost nothing, and it is the answer to the self-directed investor who pays no fee at all.
