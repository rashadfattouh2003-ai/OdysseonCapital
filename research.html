The Question

We are testing one thing: whether high net worth and accredited investors in the region are dissatisfied enough with how their liquid capital is managed to move a mandate to a systematic manager with a short track record.

That is the whole commercial risk in the business. The algorithm either has an edge or it does not, and the Ledger answers that over time. What the Ledger cannot answer is whether anyone wants to buy it, at what size, and at what fee. This instrument exists to answer that before we spend money acquiring clients.

ItemDescription
PopulationHigh net worth and accredited individuals holding investable assets they do not manage full time.
GeographyUAE first, Abu Dhabi and Dubai, with GCC expatriate investors as a secondary frame.
ModeShort self-completion survey, roughly five minutes, followed by a fifteen minute interview with anyone who opts in.
ScreeningQuestion one screens on investable assets. Respondents below the threshold are thanked and exited rather than counted.
Target sampleTo set. A qualitative read needs roughly 30 completes; a number you would price against needs several hundred.
CurrencyFigures are asked in US dollars, since the mandate reports in dollars.
StatusInstrument drafted. No responses collected. Nothing on this page is a finding.

This page is the measuring device, not the measurement. Every figure that appears here is an answer option we are offering respondents, never a result. When the field work is done, the findings go in their own section beneath this one and are dated.

02

The Instrument

Fourteen Questions

Thank you for taking part. We are a systematic trading desk researching how investors in the region manage the liquid part of their portfolio. It takes about five minutes, and your answers are anonymous unless you choose to leave an email address.

01

Roughly what value of investable assets do you hold, excluding your primary residence?

  • Under $100,000
  • $100,000 to $500,000
  • $500,000 to $1,000,000
  • $1,000,000 to $5,000,000
  • Over $5,000,000

Screening question. A and B exit the survey. The remaining bands map onto the ADGM thresholds of $500,000 for an Exempt Fund and $5,000,000 for a Qualified Investor Fund, so the answer tells us which structure a respondent could legally be sold.

02

How is that capital managed today? Select all that apply.

  • I pick and trade the positions myself
  • A private bank or wealth manager runs a discretionary portfolio
  • An automated or robo-advisor service
  • Index funds or ETFs I buy and hold
  • A fund, hedge fund or external manager
  • It sits mostly in cash or deposits

Establishes the real alternative. For this audience the most common competitor is not another fund, it is the respondent doing it themselves or leaving it in cash.

03

In the past 24 months, have you been dissatisfied with the returns, the fees or the transparency of how that capital is managed?

  • Yes
  • No

The problem question. If this comes back mostly no, there is no market and we should know that before building a sales process.

04

How often does that dissatisfaction come up?

  • Most weeks
  • Monthly
  • Each quarter, when the statement arrives
  • Once or twice a year
  • Rarely

Frequency separates a live irritation from a background one. Quarterly answers are the interesting ones, because that is the reporting cadence we would be displacing.

05

How much of a problem is this for you, where 1 is a minor annoyance and 5 is a serious problem?

  • 1
  • 2
  • 3
  • 4
  • 5

Severity. Cross-tabbed against question 4, this is what tells us whether to lead on returns or on transparency.

06

What have you done about it? Select all that apply.

  • Moved money between managers
  • Negotiated the fee down
  • Taken more of it back under my own control
  • Shifted into index funds
  • Nothing, I have left it as it is
  • Other

Switching behaviour. Someone who has already moved a mandate once will move again; someone who has done nothing for five years is expensive to convert whatever they say.

07

How satisfied are you with the way that capital is handled now, where 1 is very dissatisfied and 5 is very satisfied?

  • 1
  • 2
  • 3
  • 4
  • 5

Asked separately from question 5 on purpose. People report a problem and still report satisfaction, and the gap between the two answers is where the opportunity actually sits.

08

In the past year, roughly what did you pay in management and performance fees across that capital?

  • Nothing, I manage it myself
  • Under $2,500
  • $2,500 to $10,000
  • $10,000 to $25,000
  • Over $25,000
  • I do not know what I paid

Existing spend, and the single most useful answer in the survey. Option F is deliberate: an investor who cannot state their own fee burden is being sold to on a different basis than price.

09

Have you actively looked for a better manager or strategy in the past two years?

  • Yes, and I switched
  • Yes, but I stayed where I was
  • No

Intent. B is the warmest segment in the whole study: already shopping, not yet satisfied by anything they found.

10

What is the most frustrating part of how your capital is managed today?

Free text

The only open question in the survey, and the one we will quote in the positioning work. Placed after the scales so the respondent has already thought about it.

11

Which fee structure would seem reasonable for a strategy that performed well?

  • A flat management fee only, under 1 per cent
  • A flat management fee only, 1 to 2 per cent
  • A low management fee plus a share of the profits
  • No management fee, profit share only
  • A fixed annual retainer regardless of size

Price discovery, asked as structure rather than as a number, so the answer is not anchored by ours. We charge 1 per cent plus 12 per cent above a 12 per cent hurdle, which is option C. The question stays in the survey to test how far that sits from what this audience considers reasonable.

12

In a year where the strategy worked, what peak to trough fall in your account value would you accept along the way?

  • Under 5 per cent
  • 5 to 10 per cent
  • 10 to 20 per cent
  • 20 to 35 per cent
  • More than 35 per cent, if the end result justified it

Drawdown tolerance, and the question most likely to disqualify us honestly. The option overlay that produced both trades in the Ledger carries far more variance than the spot strategy, and a book full of A and B answers means that overlay cannot be sold to them.

13

How long a track record would a new manager need before you would allocate?

  • Under a year, if I can see the trades
  • One to two years
  • Three years
  • Five years or more
  • It depends on who else has allocated

Our hardest constraint. Odysseon has two closed positions, so answers of C and D are not addressable this year at any price. Answer A is the segment the Ledger was built for.

14

Would you be willing to speak with us for fifteen minutes, or to see the strategy run on a trial allocation? If so, please leave an email address.

Free text, optional

The conversion question, and the only one that produces a pipeline. Response rate here is the real output of the study.

What It Decides

Question To Decision

Every question above exists to settle a decision we are currently guessing at. If a question does not change what we would do, it should not be in the survey.

QuestionsDecision it settles
1Which regulatory structure we build toward, and therefore the minimum allocation.
2, 6, 9Who we are actually competing against, and how hard that incumbent is to displace.
3, 4, 5, 7Whether the pitch leads on returns or on transparency.
8, 11Whether our 1 and 12 over a 12 per cent hurdle is priced where this audience expects, and what they are paying today.
12Whether the option overlay is sellable to this audience, or stays a house strategy.
13Whether we can raise at all this year, or whether the honest answer is to keep trading and publish.
14The pipeline, and whether the study pays for itself.
